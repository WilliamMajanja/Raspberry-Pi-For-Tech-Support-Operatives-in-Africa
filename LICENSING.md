# Licensing and Provenance

The repository includes an inherited `LICENSE` file containing the text of CC0 1.0 Universal. The original tree also contains historical contributions and references to tools and projects by other authors. A license file in a repository does not, by itself, establish that its uploader owned every included work or that third-party code, prose, images, or documentation is covered by the same terms. The refreshed guide links to sources and writes original summaries; it does not copy material from the referenced books.

## Reuse guidance

- The existing `LICENSE` is preserved as inherited project metadata; this edition does not claim to have verified the authority of every historical rights holder.
- Newly written pages link to upstream sources and summarize them in original language instead of reproducing their text.
- Third-party repositories, course material, images, code, and books retain their own licenses and attribution requirements.
- International Mine Action Standards are UN-owned and copyrighted; the cited IMAS pages state their reuse conditions. POTI training materials may be provided under revocable licenses. This guide links to those sources and summarizes themes in original language; do not copy their course pages, PDFs, scenarios, figures, or standards text into the repository.
- Fork status is identified where known. A public repository is not automatically open-source, and a fork is not sole authorship.
- Before redistributing legacy excerpts or assets, verify their source, license, and required attribution with the rights holder or upstream project.

The author portfolio is a directory of links and brief factual descriptions, not a copy of the linked projects. One linked project, `atlaz`, describes itself as proprietary; the Jester-themed ransomware repository is a fork and reports no license in GitHub metadata.
