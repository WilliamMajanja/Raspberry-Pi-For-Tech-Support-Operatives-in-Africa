# Guide Structure

The guide moves from practical support to foundational concepts, then to specialist contexts. Every chapter identifies its prerequisites, evidence, verification method, and known limitations.

1. **Introduction and responsibility** — audience, scope, source standards, and working boundaries.
2. **Raspberry Pi field support** — choose an image, prepare a device, troubleshoot, protect user data, and hand over support clearly.
3. **Linux foundations and command-line recipes** — understand the kernel, Bash, storage, packages, recovery, and the effects of commonly used commands.
4. **Peace Operations field support** — support mine-action, child-protection, and WPS information flows without taking on specialist operational roles.
5. **Public-interest Informatics** — examine the Muainishi application series, health and agricultural data stewardship, corporate-accountability evidence, and how to evaluate cancer/GE-crop claims.
6. **Learn by doing** — solve Base, Quiz, and Crypto levels offline with no CTF server or extra package.
7. **CPIP- case study** — understand the Coffee Pot Internet Protocol and critically review its setup, dependencies, and security claims.
8. **FieldKit and the Coffee Pot Snake Cryptex** — use original, offline-friendly support utilities and a layered learning puzzle.
9. **Field hackathon** — scope a practical team challenge, use synthetic field-data exercises, document a safe demo, and verify a publisher-signed edition manifest.
10. **Informatics portfolio** — review the Muainishi prototypes and investigate public-health or corporate-accountability claims with provenance, uncertainty, and case-specific evidence.

Each practical recipe follows a predictable format: task, platform and prerequisites, steps, explanation, verification, safety/rollback, and sources. Examples use fictional data and local scope unless clearly labeled otherwise. Security assessments require systems the learner owns or is explicitly authorized to assess. Printable team worksheets and original diagrams support offline sessions.

The book is a static GitHub Markdown publication. It has no site generator or required build dependency; programs described in a chapter may have their own packages, hardware, or services.
