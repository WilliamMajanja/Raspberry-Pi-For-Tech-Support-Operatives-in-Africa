# Selected Security Council Resolution Reference

This is a **selected thematic reading list** for mine action, children and armed conflict (CAAC), and Women, Peace and Security (WPS). It is not an exhaustive compilation of every Security Council decision relevant to a country, mission, or incident. Check the current mission mandate and the official resolution text before operational use. The brief summaries below are original orientation notes, not quotations or legal advice.

The thematic list and the public UN resolution index were reviewed on **12 October 2026**. The Security Council also adopts country- and mission-specific decisions with relevant provisions; those are intentionally not enumerated here.

## Mine action and explosive hazards

| Resolution | Focus | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [S/RES/1325 (2000)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1325(2000)) | Women, Peace and Security | Calls for gender perspectives in peacekeeping and notes the need for mine-clearance and mine-awareness programmes to consider the specific needs of women and girls. |
| [S/RES/2365 (2017)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2365%282017%29) | Mine action | The first thematic Security Council resolution dedicated to mine action; recognizes the civilian threat from mines, ERW, and IEDs and calls for measures including risk education, victim assistance, standards, and early planning. |

Country-specific peace-operation and humanitarian mandates can include mine-action tasks, so this thematic pair is not a complete mandate list.

## Children and armed conflict

The following resolutions form the principal thematic sequence. The UN identifies six grave violations: recruitment and use; killing and maiming; rape and other sexual violence; abduction; attacks on schools and hospitals; and denial of humanitarian access.

| Resolution | Main contribution (short summary) |
| --- | --- |
| [S/RES/1261 (1999)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1261(1999)) | First Security Council resolution on children and armed conflict; places the issue on the Council’s agenda. |
| [S/RES/1314 (2000)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1314(2000)) | Develops Council attention to children’s protection in conflict and peace processes. |
| [S/RES/1379 (2001)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1379(2001)) | Requests identification/reporting of parties that recruit or use children. |
| [S/RES/1460 (2003)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1460(2003)) | Calls for an “era of application” of child-protection norms and action by listed parties. |
| [S/RES/1539 (2004)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1539(2004)) | Calls for time-bound action plans and development of a monitoring and reporting mechanism. |
| [S/RES/1612 (2005)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1612(2005)) | Establishes the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism and the Security Council Working Group on CAAC. |
| [S/RES/1882 (2009)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1882(2009)) | Adds killing and maiming and rape/sexual violence as listing triggers for parties. |
| [S/RES/1998 (2011)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1998(2011)) | Adds attacks on schools and hospitals as listing triggers. |
| [S/RES/2068 (2012)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2068(2012)) | Reaffirms action against persistent perpetrators and implementation of commitments. |
| [S/RES/2143 (2014)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2143(2014)) | Addresses military use of schools and child-protection training for peacekeepers. |
| [S/RES/2225 (2015)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2225(2015)) | Adds abduction of children as a listing trigger. |
| [S/RES/2427 (2018)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2427(2018)) | Provides a broader prevention, protection, and reintegration framework across the conflict cycle. |
| [S/RES/2601 (2021)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2601(2021)) | Focuses on protection of education and continuation of education in armed conflict. |
| [S/RES/2764 (2024)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2764(2024)) | Emphasizes sustainable child-protection capacity and coordination during UN peace-operation transitions. |

The six-violation list and country situations evolve through Secretary-General reports, action plans, and mission mandates; check the [official CAAC mandate and resources](https://childrenandarmedconflict.un.org/en/about-the-mandate/).

## Women, Peace and Security

UN Women identifies ten core thematic WPS resolutions. Resolution 2538 (2020) is a related, dedicated decision on women in peacekeeping; it is included separately because reference lists differ in whether they count it as part of the core ten.

| Resolution | Main contribution (short summary) |
| --- | --- |
| [S/RES/1325 (2000)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1325(2000)) | Foundational WPS framework: participation, gender perspectives, protection, and women’s role in peacebuilding. |
| [S/RES/1820 (2008)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1820(2008)) | Recognizes conflict-related sexual violence as a matter of international peace and security. |
| [S/RES/1888 (2009)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1888(2009)) | Strengthens UN leadership and response mechanisms on sexual violence in conflict. |
| [S/RES/1889 (2009)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1889(2009)) | Focuses on women’s participation in peacebuilding and monitoring implementation. |
| [S/RES/1960 (2010)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1960(2010)) | Establishes further monitoring and reporting measures on conflict-related sexual violence. |
| [S/RES/2106 (2013)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2106(2013)) | Emphasizes accountability and implementation of obligations on sexual violence. |
| [S/RES/2122 (2013)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2122(2013)) | Calls for consistent integration of WPS and women’s participation in peace processes. |
| [S/RES/2242 (2015)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2242(2015)) | Establishes the Informal Experts Group and advances implementation and participation. |
| [S/RES/2467 (2019)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2467(2019)) | Strengthens a survivor-centred approach to preventing and responding to conflict-related sexual violence. |
| [S/RES/2493 (2019)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2493(2019)) | Calls for full implementation of previous WPS resolutions and meaningful participation. |
| [S/RES/2538 (2020)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2538(2020)) | Dedicated resolution calling for women’s full, effective, and meaningful participation in UN peacekeeping. |

## Related protection-of-civilians references

For broader civilian-protection context, also consult [S/RES/1265 (1999)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1265(1999)), [S/RES/1296 (2000)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1296(2000)), [S/RES/1674 (2006)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1674(2006)), and [S/RES/1894 (2009)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/1894(2009)). For the safety and security of humanitarian personnel and UN/associated personnel, see [S/RES/2730 (2024)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2730(2024)). These are not substitutes for the applicable country mandate or humanitarian law.

## How to use this reference

1. Read the operative paragraphs of the resolution, not only a summary.
2. Check the mission mandate, host-state framework, and current UN policy that apply to the location and role.
3. Distinguish Security Council resolutions from General Assembly resolutions, treaties, International Mine Action Standards, and training-course material; they have different status and scope.
4. Record the document identifier and date in support plans. Review amendments and later decisions before a deployment.
