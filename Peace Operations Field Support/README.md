# Peace Operations Field Support

This chapter connects technical support practice with the protection, data, and coordination responsibilities that can arise in peace-support, humanitarian, and civilian-protection contexts. It is an orientation for technical support operatives—not operational training for peacekeepers, deminers, explosive-ordnance-disposal (EOD) teams, child-protection officers, or gender advisers.

The key operational lesson is to support qualified people and established procedures with dependable, secure, accessible information systems. A Raspberry Pi, database, communications link, or dashboard must not replace an accredited mine-action authority, trained child-protection specialist, or mission chain of command.

## High-impact practices covered

- **Evidence-led information management:** record source, time, confidence, consent, access limits, and changes; maintain provenance and retention controls.
- **Inclusive participation:** make information systems and risk messages accessible to women, men, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, and local communities in languages they use.
- **Privacy and do-no-harm:** minimize personal data, restrict access, avoid publishing sensitive locations or case details, and use approved referral channels.
- **Continuity and handover:** preserve support capacity and secure records through staff, service, or mission transitions.
- **Role boundaries:** support technical infrastructure without attempting explosive-hazard identification, clearance, interviewing children, or case decisions.
